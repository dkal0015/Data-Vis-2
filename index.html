THE NATIONAL PICTURE

A familiar pastime.
A different story in every state.

About one in seven Australians rode in the week before the 2025 survey.

The Australian Capital Territory recorded the highest weekly participation at 20.0%; South Australia recorded 11.8%. These are shares of each jurisdiction’s population, so a large state does not automatically rank higher.

+5.1 points

The ACT’s weekly rate sits above the national rate of 14.9%.

A darker shade means a larger share of people riding. Change the recall window to compare occasional participation with weekly riding. ACT’s label is offset for legibility.

PEOPLE BEHIND THE PEDALS

The route matters.
So does feeling welcome on it.

Participation varies by age and gender. For the riders already taking part, avoiding busy roads is a common preference—an important reason to look beyond on-road cycling.

A TRAIL FOR EVERY RIDE

Rail corridors.
New possibilities.

Rail trails turn former railway corridors into recreational routes. This selection of 14 published listings across six states shows the variety: short coastal rides, sealed regional routes and longer gravel journeys.

A selection, not a census. These trails were chosen to illustrate different regions, lengths and surfaces. The selection cannot tell us which state has the largest cycling network or the most trail users. ACT and NT are not represented.

READING THE SURFACE

Know the surface before you compare.

  • Sealed

    Only asphalt, bitumen, concrete or sealed tags are listed.

  • Mixed

    At least one sealed and one unsealed surface tag are listed.

  • Unsealed

    Only gravel or compacted earth tags are listed.

Groups are derived from published tags. They describe the presence of a surface, not the proportion of paved kilometres or riding difficulty.

THE NEXT KILOMETRE

Occasional rides.
Everyday potential.

A large annual riding population does not mean everyone rides every week.

The ACT has the highest annual and weekly participation in 2025. The selected trails demonstrate recreational possibilities, while the survey shows that quieter routes appeal to many riders.

Together, the sources suggest why inviting routes matter. They do not prove that longer trail networks cause higher participation. Understanding that link would need a complete network inventory and comparable measures of use.

A second view of participation

AusPlay estimates 2.049 million Australians aged 15+ participated in cycling, including BMX and mountain biking, at least once in 2025. Its sport and physical activity measure differs from CWANZ’s all-age travel survey, so the two totals are not combined.

SOURCES & METHODS

Behind the ride.

01 / Participation

CWANZ · National Walking and Cycling Participation Survey 2025, prepared by Painted Dog Research. Data: pp. 47, 49, 51, 60, 76, 127 and 130. Fieldwork: 11 March–9 April 2025. The report covers 4,796 households and 12,386 persons; some cycling tables use a base of 12,186. Use each chart’s cited base.

Adults report for their household, including children. Responses are weighted and self-reported. Survey mode, recall, weather and rounding affect estimates. Historical participation values use the revised 2025 report series; the weighting changes were selectively applied, with detailed discussion on pp. 12–13.

Download participation CSV ↓

02 / Trails & geography

Rail Trails Australia: 14 individually researched listing lengths, short surface tags and location coordinates, accessed 15 October 2026. Source links and status for every listing are in the trail CSV and the table below. This is a selected sample, not a representative inventory; no RTA map artwork or route geometry is reproduced.

Route geometry: OpenStreetMap relation 29695, version 75, last edited 5 October 2026. Its 94 member ways retain original node coordinates and are not connected artificially. © OpenStreetMap contributors; ODbL 1.0. Boundaries: Natural Earth, 1:50m admin 1, public domain. National maps use an equal-area conic projection.

03 / Context & creation

Australian Sports Commission · Cycling: AusPlay 2025 adult estimate. Context only, with a different population and measure. It is not used to calculate CWANZ rates.
